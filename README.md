### Decontamination and Decommissioning (DandD)
The Decontamination and Decommissioning (DandD) software package, developed by Sandia National Laboratories for the U.S. Nuclear Regulatory Commission (NRC), is a user-friendly analytical tool that lets NRC licensees demonstrate (and the NRC verify) that residual soil or building contamination remaining after decontamination and decommissioning complies with the radiological dose criteria for license termination in 10 CFR Part 20, Subpart E (NRC, 1997). It was designed to simplify decommissioning where low levels of contamination exist.

DandD embodies the NRC's screening methodology for converting residual radioactivity levels at a site into annual dose, using reasonably conservative scenarios, fate and transport models, and default parameter values that allow the NRC to quantitatively estimate the risk of releasing a site from only the level of contamination. A licensee may run the code with these default values, or substitute site-specific values where such information is available. DandD evaluates dose for four scenarios: residential, building occupancy, building renovation, and drinking water. The screening methodology is part of a larger decision framework, documented in the NRC's technical guidance for decommissioning, that encourages licensees to optimize their choice of actions at a site, including collecting additional data and information.
